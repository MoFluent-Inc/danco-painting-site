Cabinet painting and staining

Cabinet Painting and Staining Questions

How long does a cabinet painting project take?

On-site labor commonly runs 3 to 5 days for a small kitchen with 10 to 15 cabinet openings, 4 to 7 days for a medium kitchen, and 7 to 10 days for a large kitchen with 20 or more openings, covering prep, priming, topcoats, and dry time between steps.

What's the difference between painting and staining cabinets?

Painting is prep-heavy: disassembly, degreasing, deglossing or sanding, priming, and two topcoats, roughly six to eight steps. Staining bare or stripped wood is shorter, a light sand, stain, sealer coat, and topcoat, about three steps. Gel stain is a common middle option for cabinets that already have some finish, since it sits on the surface instead of penetrating into the wood.

Why does the primer matter so much on cabinets?

Factory cabinet finishes are hard and slick, so new paint won't mechanically bond to an intact glossy film without deglossing and the right bonding primer first. Skipping that step is the most common reason cabinet paint chips or peels within the first one to two years.

How soon can I use my kitchen normally after cabinets are painted?

Cabinets are usually safe for light use, like opening and closing doors, around 7 days. Avoid heavy use like stacking dishes directly on painted surfaces for 14 days, and expect full hardness at 21 to 30 days depending on the product. Dry to the touch is not the same as fully cured.

Will the wood grain show through the paint?

On closed-grain woods like maple or MDF slab doors, generally no. On open-grain oak, yes, unless a grain filler is used first to fill the pores before priming. This is worth discussing before the job starts since it changes the prep scope.

Exterior painting

Exterior Painting Questions

Why does the house need to dry out before it gets painted?

Power washing is usually the first step in an exterior repaint, and the surface needs a real dry-out period, commonly 24 to 48 hours, before scraping, caulking, or painting starts. Painting over damp or wet siding is one of the most cited causes of premature peeling.

Does the weather affect when you can paint my house?

Yes. Exterior paint generally needs air and surface temperatures in roughly the 50-85°F range, and the surface should stay at least 5°F above the dew point during application and for about 48 hours after, since condensation on a not-yet-cured film prevents proper adhesion.

When is the best time of year to paint a house exterior in Oklahoma?

Spring and fall are generally the best windows nationally, since they land in a moderate-temperature, lower-humidity range. Given Bristow's humid climate, summer work is still workable if it's scheduled around the hottest part of the day and the shaded side of the house.

Why is my paint peeling in large sheets instead of small flakes?

Peeling in sheets, rather than small chips, often points to moisture getting in from behind the paint film, from a leaky gutter, a failed caulk joint, or rotted wood, not from the weather side. It's worth inspecting flashing, gutters, and caulk before repainting rather than just scraping and repainting the visible symptom.

Interior painting

Interior Painting Questions

Will you be able to see where a wall was patched?

Only if the patch isn't handled correctly. Drywall compound absorbs paint differently than the surrounding wall, so an unprimed patch can dry with a different sheen, and a smooth patch on a textured wall can show under angled light even with a perfect color match. Spot-priming and texture-matching the patch first prevents both problems.

What paint sheen should I choose for each room?

It depends on the room. Flat or matte works best on ceilings because it hides surface imperfections. Eggshell or satin is a good balance for general living spaces. Kitchens and bathrooms do better with satin or semi-gloss since the tighter film resists moisture better. Trim and doors usually get semi-gloss or gloss for durability and easy cleaning.

How long until my walls are fully cured, not just dry?

Interior paint is typically dry to the touch in 30 to 60 minutes and safe to recoat in a few hours, but full cure, the point of maximum hardness, isn't reached for roughly 3 to 4 weeks. Give it more than a day or two before hanging pictures or pushing furniture tight against a wall.

Is interior paint safe with kids or pets in the house?

Low- and zero-VOC paints meaningfully reduce off-gassing, with most emissions happening in the first 24 to 72 hours on low-emission products. This is worth raising if anyone in the home has asthma or a chemical sensitivity, or if small children or pets will be back in the room soon after painting.

Epoxy flooring

Epoxy Flooring Questions

Why does a garage floor need a moisture test before epoxy goes down?

Concrete moisture is a leading cause of epoxy failure. Moisture testing checks whether vapor is moving up through the slab. Coating over a slab that hasn't been tested can trap that moisture underneath the film and cause bubbling or delamination later, regardless of how well the surface was prepped.

How long before I can park in my garage again?

Foot traffic is commonly safe around 24 hours after application, vehicle traffic needs 48 to 72 hours, and full cure with maximum chemical and abrasion resistance takes 5 to 7 days. Polyaspartic systems cure faster than standard epoxy and can sometimes allow vehicle traffic within 24 hours.

What is hot tire pickup?

It's when a coating bubbles, lifts, or peels specifically where a car's tires park, because a hot tire, which can reach roughly 140°F, softens a weak coating film and then grabs and pulls at it as it cools. Thin, low-solids coatings are the most vulnerable; genuine 100% solids epoxy or polyaspartic systems are formulated to resist it.

Can you coat a floor that already has cracks in it?

Yes, but the crack treatment matters. Static, non-moving cracks get ground open and filled with a rigid epoxy filler. Control joints and expansion joints are different and need a flexible filler, since they're engineered to keep moving with the slab. The wrong filler type lets the crack or joint pattern show through the finished coating.

Power washing

Power Washing Questions

Can you pressure wash my roof?

No, not with a pressure washer. High-pressure water strips the protective granules off asphalt shingles, which shortens the roof's service life. Roof algae and moss should be treated with a soft wash, a low-pressure chemical treatment, combined with gentle mechanical debris removal instead.

What's the difference between pressure washing and soft washing?

Pressure washing uses high mechanical force and is appropriate for durable, non-porous surfaces like concrete and brick. Soft washing uses low pressure combined with a cleaning solution that does the actual work chemically, and it's the correct method for painted siding, roof shingles, stucco, and other surfaces that high pressure can damage.

Why won't plain water get rid of the stains on my driveway?

Oklahoma's red clay soil is high in iron oxide and fine clay that wedges into concrete pores, a mineral bond rather than surface dirt. Power washing with water alone usually can't lift it. It typically needs an acid-based cleaner with dwell time, similar to how a rust stain from sprinkler overspray needs treatment.

Do I need power washing before you paint my house?

Yes, for any exterior repaint. It's the first prep step, not an optional add-on, and it needs a genuine dry-out period, commonly 24 to 48 hours, before scraping and painting can start.
