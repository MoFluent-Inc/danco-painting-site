What to look for

What Quality Work Actually Looks Like

Here is what a well-done job in each service actually involves, and what a finished result should look like when the prep work behind it was done right.

Cabinet painting and staining

What a quality cabinet job looks like

Cabinet work is prep-heavy. Most of the time on a good job goes into degreasing, deglossing, and priming before any color goes on, since new paint can't grip a slick factory finish without that step. In a job done right, the finish holds without chipping where doors meet the frame, oak grain is either filled smooth or shows evenly on purpose, and doors close without sticking, a sign they went back on only after the finish fully cured, not just after it felt dry.

Exterior painting

What a quality exterior repaint looks like

A good exterior repaint starts with a real dry-out period after any power washing, since coating over damp siding is one of the most common causes of early peeling. Look for a consistent sheen across the wall with no lap marks or streaking, smooth and continuous caulk lines at trim and window edges, and no bubbling where the wall took direct sun during application.

Interior painting

What a quality interior job looks like

The tell for interior work is usually the patches. A wall repair that was spot-primed and texture-matched before the finish coat disappears into the surrounding wall, even under angled light. A patch that skipped that step shows up as a slightly different sheen or a flat spot on a textured wall. Cut-in lines at corners and trim should blend into the rolled field with no visible seam.

Epoxy flooring

What a quality epoxy floor looks like

A properly installed epoxy floor starts with a moisture-tested, mechanically ground slab, not just a quick acid etch. Look for an even coating with no bubbling or cloudy haze, which can signal a moisture or humidity problem during application, evenly distributed flake or texture if that was part of the job, and no lifting where tires typically park.

Power washing

What a quality power washing job looks like

The right method depends on the surface. Siding, stucco, and roof shingles should be soft washed with a low-pressure chemical treatment, not blasted with high pressure, which can strip paint, crack siding, or strip the protective granules off shingles. Concrete and brick can take real pressure, but should come out even, with no zipper-mark streaking from an inconsistent pass.
