Cabinet Painting and Staining

Cabinet refinishing is a prep-heavy job. Most of the time goes into cleaning, deglossing, and priming, not the topcoat, because that sequence decides whether the finish holds for years or peels within months. Danco handles the full process, from labeling doors before they come off to cure time before they go back on.

More Painting and Surface Services

Exterior Painting

A repaint starts with a wash, then a real dry-out window, commonly 24 to 48 hours, before any scraping or painting begins. Loose paint gets scraped to a stable edge, rotted wood gets repaired, gaps get caulked, and bare or repaired areas get primed before the topcoat goes on.

Learn more about exterior painting

Interior Painting

Every room moves through the same core steps: clean the walls, patch and repair, match texture on patches so they disappear, then prime the patched areas specifically before the finish coat. Sheen is chosen room by room, flat for ceilings, satin or semi-gloss where moisture and cleaning matter more.

Learn more about interior painting

Epoxy Flooring

Epoxy flooring is a concrete job before it's a coating job. The slab gets tested for moisture, ground to open its pores, and checked for cracks and joints that need their own treatment before any color or topcoat goes down.

Learn more about epoxy flooring

Power Washing

Pressure washing and soft washing are different tools for different surfaces. Concrete and brick can take real mechanical force; painted siding, stucco, and roof shingles need a lower-pressure, chemical-based soft wash instead.

Why it holds up

Why Prep Decides How Long a Job Lasts

Across every service Danco offers, the pattern is the same: most early failures trace back to a skipped prep step, not the paint or coating itself.

  • Cabinets. Skipping deglossing on a factory-finished door is the single most common reason paint chips or peels within the first year or two.
  • Exterior. Painting over siding that looks dry but isn't, right after a wash, is one of the most cited causes of early peeling.
  • Interior. An unprimed drywall patch dries to a different sheen than the wall around it, a defect that shows up under any angled light.
  • Epoxy. Uncontrolled moisture under a slab is why grinding and moisture testing come before color, not after.
  • Power washing. Using pressure-wash force on siding, stucco, or a shingle roof can strip paint, crack material, or remove the granules that protect a roof from UV.
