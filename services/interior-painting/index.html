Interior Painting

Interior painting looks like a color job, but the part that determines whether it looks finished is patch work: spot priming a repair and matching its texture to the surrounding wall so it disappears instead of reading as a rectangle under angled light.

The prep sequence

Danco's interior prep sequence

  1. Protect and clear the room. Furniture moved and covered, floors covered with drop cloths, wall hangings and outlet covers removed.
  2. Clean the walls. A mild soap-and-water wipe-down removes dust and grease, especially in kitchens, before any patching or painting, since paint applied over dirt bonds to the dirt, not the wall.
  3. Patch and repair. Holes and dents get filled with spackle or drywall compound and allowed to dry, a few hours for small patches, longer or multiple thin coats for larger repairs so the fill does not shrink and crack.
  4. Sand. Medium grit knocks down dried patch compound and rough spots, followed by dust removal before priming.
  5. Spot-prime the patch, not just paint over it. Drywall compound absorbs paint at a different rate than the surrounding wall, so an unprimed patch dries with a different sheen, a defect called flashing. A dedicated primer over the repaired area, then a full finish coat over the whole wall, is what prevents it.
  6. Cut in, then roll while the edge is still wet. A dried brush edge and a freshly rolled field show a visible seam if they are not blended while both are wet.
  7. Match roller nap to wall texture. A short nap on smooth drywall gives even coverage without adding unwanted stipple; a longer nap is needed on genuinely textured surfaces to reach into texture valleys.

Where interior patch work gets skilled

Matching texture, not just color

Orange peel texture is comparatively easy to blend into an existing wall. Knockdown's irregular, flattened-splatter pattern is harder to match because it varies from house to house, and even wall to wall. A patch that needs to disappear gets skim-coated smooth, allowed to dry, then re-textured: thinned joint compound applied with a textured roller and feathered in for orange peel, or the same splatter step followed by a knockdown pass roughly 10 to 20 minutes later for knockdown, timing that shifts with humidity and room temperature.

A patch that skips this step and gets painted smooth is visible under any raking light even when the paint color is a perfect match, which is why it is worth explaining to a customer up front rather than after the fact.

Product selection

Choosing the right sheen for each room

Ceilings

Flat or matte is standard because it hides surface imperfections, uneven texture, and minor drywall seams, rather than reflecting light off them the way a higher sheen would.

Living spaces and bedrooms

Eggshell or satin balances a washable, scuff-resistant surface against a finish that still hides wall imperfections reasonably well.

Kitchens and bathrooms

Satin or semi-gloss is preferred because the tighter, smoother film resists moisture saturation better than flat or eggshell, which matters where steam and repeated cleaning are routine.

Trim and doors

Semi-gloss or gloss for durability and easy cleaning, consistent with the trim enamel products used on cabinet projects.

Safety in older housing stock

Older homes: lead paint and popcorn ceilings

Under the EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting Rule, any paid work that disturbs six square feet or more of painted surface per room in housing built before 1978 requires a firm certified under the rule and the use of lead-safe work practices, unless there is documentation the existing paint is not lead-based. Minor repairs disturbing six square feet or less per room are exempt. This applies to scraping, sanding, and heavy patching of old paint, not to painting over an intact, undisturbed surface.

Homes built before 1980, and in some cases as late as 1985, may have asbestos-containing popcorn ceiling texture. An intact, undisturbed ceiling poses minimal risk, but scraping, sanding, or skim-coating it without testing first is where it becomes a real hazard, since fibers are invisible and only dangerous once airborne. Bristow's older housing stock makes this a real, not theoretical, consideration on some interior jobs, and it gets flagged during the initial walkthrough rather than assumed away.

Indoor air quality

Indoor air quality during the job

Low-VOC paint is defined as under 50 grams per liter for flat products or under 150 grams per liter for non-flat; zero-VOC sits under 5 grams per liter in the base before tinting, though colorants added at the counter can reintroduce some VOCs even into a zero-VOC base. Off-gassing is heaviest in the first 24 to 72 hours for low-emission products and can continue at a low level for up to six months on higher-VOC products. This is worth raising proactively on any occupied-home job with small children, pets, or a resident with asthma or a chemical sensitivity, since it affects both product choice and how soon the family should fully re-occupy the room.

What to expect

Timeline and re-occupancy

Standard interior latex, such as Behr Premium Plus, is dry-to-touch in about an hour and recoat-ready in about 4 hours, broadly consistent with the 30-to-60-minute touch and 2-to-4-hour recoat range cited across waterborne wall products at 70°F and 50% relative humidity. A single room with normal prep is typically a one-day job for paint application once patch-dry time is accounted for. Labor, not paint, is 70 to 85% of a job's total cost, so prep-heavy walls with a lot of patching or a dark-to-light color change cost more per square foot than a same-color refresh even at identical square footage.

Rooms are usable again within the recoat window, but hanging pictures, pushing furniture tight against a wall, or heavy cleaning should wait a day or two longer, and full hardness is not reached for roughly 3 to 4 weeks.

What homeowners ask

Interior painting FAQ

What does an interior repaint typically cost?

Across the industry, interior painting runs roughly $2 to $6 per square foot of floor area, with a single room typically $350 to $850 and a full-house interior repaint typically $3,500 to $10,000 depending on square footage, ceiling height, and existing wall condition. These figures reflect broad industry data, not a fixed price for your rooms.

Can we still use the room while it dries?

Yes for light use once dry-to-touch, a few hours, but full cure takes 3 to 4 weeks, so heavy scrubbing, tape, or pressing furniture tight against a wall should wait.

Will the patch show?

Only if it is not spot-primed and texture-matched. This is the most common source of "I can still see where you patched it" complaints, and it is a prep-sequencing issue, not a paint-quality issue.

Is the paint safe with kids, pets, or allergies in the house?

Low- or zero-VOC products meaningfully reduce off-gassing, with most emission happening in the first 24 to 72 hours on low-emission products versus up to six months on higher-VOC products.

How long does a typical room or house take?

A single room with normal prep is typically a one-day job for the paint application itself. A full-house repaint scales with square footage and wall condition, and labor, not paint, drives most of the cost difference between jobs.
