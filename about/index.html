What that actually means on a job

Attention to Detail, By Service

"Attention to detail" is easy to say and hard to prove. Here's what it looks like in the sequence Danco follows on each type of project.

Cabinet painting and staining

Doors and drawer fronts come off first, each one numbered to its original opening, since factory hinge mortises rarely interchange. Every factory finish gets degreased and deglossed before primer goes on, the step that decides whether paint holds for years or peels within months.

Exterior painting

A wash needs a real dry-out window, commonly 24 to 48 hours, before scraping or painting starts. Coating over damp siding is one of the most common causes of early paint failure, so the schedule follows the weather, not the calendar.

Interior painting

A patched wall gets its own coat of primer before the topcoat, not just a coat of paint over the patch. Skipping that step is the most common reason a repaired spot shows through as a dull or shiny patch under angled light, even when the color matches.

Epoxy flooring

Concrete gets tested for internal moisture, not just checked by eye, before anything is coated. Nationally, inadequate surface prep, not the product, is the most cited reason epoxy floors fail early, which is why grinding and testing come before color.

Power washing

Pressure washing and soft washing are different tools for different surfaces, and picking the wrong one is the most common way this service goes wrong. Concrete and brick can take real mechanical force; painted siding and roof shingles need a gentler, chemical-based soft wash instead.
