A New-Construction, HOA-Governed Suburb

Jenks grew from 9,557 people at the 2000 census to 16,926 in 2010, a 76.7% increase, then to 25,949 by 2020, one of the fastest growth rates of any Oklahoma city over that period. The Oklahoma Historical Society credits much of that growth through the 1980s and 1990s to the reputation of Jenks Public Schools, and that draw has continued into the 2000s and 2010s.

Building-permit activity tells the construction story: Jenks issued as many as 438 permits in a single year (2005) and has stayed active with 120 to 129 permits a year in 2024 and 2025. That is a very different pace from Bristow, where the entire annual permit count in those same two years was 28 to 50 buildings. Point2Homes reports a median construction year around 2005 for Jenks housing, which lines up with that permit history, though we treat that figure as a general indicator rather than an exact number. Only about 0.7 to 1.1 percent of Jenks homes predate 1940, a sharp contrast with Bristow's oil-boom-era core. Current median home value is reported at $327,100, well above the Tulsa metro median of $221,700.

The Oklahoma Aquarium opened along the Arkansas River in 2003, part of a riverfront push that turned Jenks from a small railroad and antique-shopping town into a regional destination. That commercial growth sits alongside, not instead of, the school-driven residential expansion above.

Yorktown, Southern Reserve, and Jenks' Other Named Communities

Yorktown is a master-planned community built out by several builders, with a pool and water-park complex, a private park, sport courts, and a stocked fishing pond. Southern Reserve is a 443-home community built between 2008 and 2018, with two ponds, a walking trail, a pool, and a dog park. Other named neighborhoods in our research include Hickory Creek Estates, Copperfield Estates, Oak Ridge, Stone Bluff Estates, Stone Creek, Fountain Ridge, Hidden Oaks, Churchill Park, Augusta Greens, Somerset, and Elm Ridge, Jenks' newest gated community, along with the Jenks Riverwalk district. NewHomeSource lists 9 gated communities in Jenks with 88 floor plans from 7 builders.

Check Your HOA Before You Change a Color

Given how many of Jenks' communities are gated and HOA-managed, an architectural-approval step before repainting should be treated as routine, not exceptional. Southern Reserve's HOA is explicit about it: all exterior modifications, including painting, must be approved by its Architectural Committee, submitted on an Architectural Review Form, before work begins. The City of Jenks does not regulate private HOA covenants itself. Those are private agreements filed with the Tulsa County Clerk, so there is no single citywide paint-color rule, only a patchwork of individual HOA requirements that vary by neighborhood. Check your covenant, or ask us and we can help you figure out what your HOA needs.

Jenks Questions

Does my Jenks HOA need to approve my paint color first?

Many Jenks communities are HOA-managed, and the rules vary by neighborhood. Southern Reserve, for example, requires Architectural Committee approval and a submitted Architectural Review Form before any exterior color change. Check your HOA's covenant before starting.

How far is Jenks from Danco's shop in Bristow?

About 31 miles via I-44 West, roughly 33 minutes without stops.

Does Danco work on newer Jenks homes?

Yes. Jenks' housing stock is largely newer construction, so most of our work here is first or second exterior repaint cycles rather than restoration, alongside interior painting for new builds.
