Housing stock

Two Housing Stories, One City

Broken Arrow is Oklahoma's 4th most populous city, growing 14.8 percent from 2010 to 2020, one of the fastest-growing incorporated municipalities in the country, with population near 123,000.

Downtown Broken Arrow, the Rose District, has a genuine early 20th-century core: Craftsman bungalows and updated traditional cottages alongside newly built urban lofts along Main Street. The Broken Arrow Hotel (built 1903, 400 S Main) and other Main Street buildings date from 1901 to 1911.

Outside that small downtown core, the story flips. Central Broken Arrow is dominated by mid-century split-levels and solid brick ranch homes built in the 1970s and 1980s. Newer southeast-side developments run later still: Forest Ridge, described by its own developer as the city's largest and most diverse community, spans construction from the early 1990s through brand-new homes today. New construction has continued at volume, with 571 new housing starts recorded in 2019 alone, a 25 percent increase over 2018, and the city is currently building the 90-acre Arrow Forge Innovation District, a new mixed-use tract with multiple housing types.

Two paint jobs, not one

Two Different Paint Jobs

Rose District and downtown

A genuine early-1900s building stock, bungalows, cottages, and a few buildings dating to 1903 through 1911, whose age creates the same kind of maintenance-repaint need seen in older neighborhoods generally.

Everywhere else in the city

A large volume of 1970s-through-present subdivision construction, with 571 new housing starts in 2019 alone, meaning a steady supply of homes reaching their first or second repaint cycle rather than restoration-grade work.

Climate is the same across both: humid subtropical with about 43.7 inches of annual precipitation, heaviest in May, and a wide seasonal swing from an average January low near 38.5°F to an average July high near 83.4°F, standard exterior-paint wear conditions for wood and brick trim regardless of the home's age.

Design rules to know

HOA and Downtown Design Rules to Know

Downtown Broken Arrow is regulated by the Downtown Residential Overlay District (DROD), adopted and updated in 2019, which governs lot, site, building-form, and building-activation standards within the Elm Place, Houston Street, 9th Street (Lynn Lane), and Kenosha Street boundary. This overlay is primarily aimed at new construction and redevelopment massing and form, eight permitted building forms, not at repainting existing homes; the publicly available overview does not specify an exterior paint-color rule.

Outside downtown, city rules step back and covenant paperwork takes over. Broken Arrow communities such as The Reserve at Battle Creek, Southbrook, and Forest Ridge's sub-neighborhoods commonly hold recorded covenants, by-laws, and architectural-application paperwork covering exterior changes, and the city's own Homeowners Associations page confirms that these groups set guidelines alongside landscaping and common-area upkeep. Central Broken Arrow bucks the pattern: it carries no mandatory HOA at all, so owners there pick exterior colors without asking anyone's permission.

There's no Danco office in Broken Arrow. The crew is based in Bristow and drives out for every job.

Neighborhoods at a glance

Named Areas of Broken Arrow

  • Rose District / Downtown: early 20th-century Craftsman bungalows and cottages mixed with new urban infill, along Main Street.
  • Central Broken Arrow: 1970s-1980s brick ranch and split-level homes on larger lots, no mandatory HOA.
  • Battle Creek: established southeast-side neighborhood; The Reserve at Battle Creek (164 homes) is HOA-governed with recorded architectural-review covenants.
  • Forest Ridge: the city's largest master-planned community, spanning early-1990s construction through current new-build phases, including sub-neighborhoods like The Highlands.
  • Southbrook: newer development with an active HOA and community amenities such as greenbelts, a pool, and sports courts.
  • Silvertree and Haikey Creek: custom-built brick and modern-farmhouse-style homes on larger southeast-side lots.

Broken Arrow Painting Questions

Does my Broken Arrow HOA need to approve my exterior paint color?

It depends on the neighborhood. Communities like The Reserve at Battle Creek, Southbrook, and Forest Ridge's sub-sections have recorded architectural-review requirements. Central Broken Arrow has no mandatory HOA, so no approval is required there.
